## 1. Forgotten check-out was stealing the next day's check-in

This was corrupting attendance data, so I fixed it first.

`getMobileAttendanceContext` looked back a flat **36 hours** for any row with a
check-in and no check-out, and offered it for check-out — with no reference to when
the shift actually ended:

```
Mon 10:00  check in
Mon 18:00  shift ends, no punch out
Tue 09:55  employee opens the app to check IN
           -> the 36h window finds Monday's open row
           -> the button says "Check out"
           -> Tuesday morning is written as MONDAY's check-out
```

Monday becomes a 24-hour shift and Tuesday has no attendance at all. Exactly what
you described.

**Now:** a duty day stays open only until **shift end + 3 hours**
(`policy.autoCloseOpenPunchAfterMinutes`, configurable). Past that the day is closed
with **`checkOut` left NULL** — the system never invents a punch time — and flagged
`missingCheckOut` so HR can regularize it and payroll treats it correctly.

Verified on a 10:00–18:00 shift:

| Punch attempt | Result |
|---|---|
| Mon 18:20 | offers Check out |
| Mon 20:40 | offers Check out (inside grace) |
| Mon 21:30 | day auto-closed → offers **Check in** |
| Tue 09:55 | day auto-closed → offers **Check in** |

Overnight shifts still work: they close after *their* shift end, not at midnight.

An hourly sweep (`:15` past each hour) closes stale days even if the employee never
opens the app again, so payroll never sees a dangling row.

**Friendly confirmations**, varied by hour rather than one flat string:

- `Good morning, Sukesh — you're checked in. Have a good shift.`
- `Attendance captured, Sukesh. See you tomorrow, on time.`
- `Attendance captured, Sukesh. Have a great night, see you tomorrow.`

## 2. Work From Home is now invisible without access

`canApplyWfh` already gated the request button, but the words leaked in five other
places: the Leave page eyebrow (`LEAVE · WFH · COMP OFF · LOP`), the request history
label, the approval list, the attendance calendar legend, the attendance override
dropdown, the account quick-action, and two dashboard widget labels.

One helper, `canSeeWfh(user, employee)` in `utils/permissions.js`, now gates all of
them. It returns true only when the employee can actually apply, **or** the viewer
administers it for others (Super Admin, HR Admin, or LEAVE approve/manage) — those
people must still be able to read and decide other people's requests.

An employee without access now sees `LEAVE · COMP OFF · LOP`, no WFH chip in the
legend, no WFH option in the override, and `Leave & Time Off` on their account page.

## 3. The sun now travels, and the moon comes out

Previously the sun sat in one fixed spot with only its fill recoloured — which is why
it never looked like the time of day had changed.

There is now a `CELESTIAL` table giving each period its own position, size, glow and
intensity:

| Period | Height | Core | Halo | Intensity | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dawn | low | 26 | 74 | .78 | cool, just risen |
| morning | rising | 32 | 92 | .92 | fresh, hard light |
| midday | highest | 40 | 128 | **1.0** | blazing, white-hot centre |
| afternoon | sinking | 35 | 96 | **.74** | dimmed and gone gold |
| evening | at horizon | 30 | 84 | .66 | setting, burnt orange |
| night | — | 25 | 52 | .95 | crescent moon |

Hard light (`rays`) is drawn only when the sun is high, which is what actually reads
as *blazing at midday* versus *hazy in the afternoon*.

At night: a **crescent moon** with craters and a cool moonlight bloom, a field of
slowly twinkling stars, and **glow-worms** drifting over the foreground from evening
onward, each on its own timing offset so they never pulse in unison.

The moon needed a second attempt. The first version stacked a dark disc over a bright
one, and when rendered it read as a dark ball rather than a crescent. It is now cut
with an SVG mask, which keeps the lit limb clean at any size.
